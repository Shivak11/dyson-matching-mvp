# Handover: Dyson store-photo matching MVP

Project folder: this folder (`dyson-mvp/`). The full plan is in `.engineering/super-outer/product-brief.md`. Read it first. The current approach review and pending recommendations are in `docs/approach-review-2026-10-15.md`.

## What this is for

On 15 October 2026 Shiva advised the AI CoE capstone team (Prince Vishwari, Vipul Tyagi; Multiplier). Their app matches store photos of Dyson products against catalog images with Gemini, and it fails in three ways:

- lookalike models get the same score (Big Ball vs V8, V6 vs V7, V12 vs V15), and their 80 to 90% threshold matched different products as one model;
- a photo with several products is judged as one picture, so the second product is never matched;
- one catalog photo per product does not cover store angles.

Shiva promised them findings by early next week (Monday or Tuesday). The deliverable is a working demo plus a one-page accuracy report. Their developers will rebuild it in their own app.

## Decisions Shiva made (do not reopen)

- Use current vision models. No training of any kind: models keep changing and the team has thousands of products. A new product is added by adding catalog photos. A newer model is adopted by rerunning the saved test set.
- Dyson only. TV and washing machines come later.
- Start now with public images. Rerun on the team's images when Prince emails them.
- When unsure, the system says "not sure" and gives its top two guesses. "Not in catalog" is an allowed answer.
- Photos per day are unknown, so measure and report the cost per photo.
- The earlier Jev exclusion assumed text-only input. Shiva reopened its possible role on 15 October 2026. Current TypeSafe documentation describes experimental image input; adding Jev remains under discussion, with no pipeline change approved.

## Pipeline to build

1. **Find.** Gemini 3.8 Flash (`gemini-3.8-flash`) returns `box_2d` for every product in the store photo. Coordinates are `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]` on a 0 to 1000 scale. Crop each box.
2. **Shortlist.** Embed each crop and every catalog image with Gemini Embedding 2. Two docs pages disagree on the ID (`gemini-embedding-2` and `gemini-embedding-2-preview`), so confirm it with one live call. The limit is 6 images per request. Keep the 3 closest products, scoring each product by its best catalog image.
3. **Check.** One call sees the crop beside each shortlisted product's catalog images. It returns a product, "not sure" with the top two, or "not in catalog", plus a confidence and the visible details it relied on. Run this step on Gemini 4 Argon (`gemini-4-argon`), GPT-5.5 (`openai/gpt-5.5`) and GPT-6.1 Sol (`openai/gpt-6.1-sol`) over the same crops, through OpenRouter or the native APIs.
4. **Variant.** Add a written "telling details" card per product, generated by comparing each product with its lookalikes. Run it as one variant of the check, so the report shows whether it helps. Do not make it the method; Shiva asked for the approach to be researched, not copied from the call.
5. **Baseline first.** Before building steps 1 to 4, reproduce the team's current method on the same test set: the whole photo against one catalog image, with a Gemini similarity score. This is the "before" number, and it proves the test set catches the reported failure.

Runner-up stack, for reference only: SAM 3 plus DINOv3 on a rented GPU.

Evidence behind the choice: Cat2Real (arXiv 2607.09888, July 2026) reports Gemini-Embedding-2 at 68.7% first-choice and 89.3% top-five on catalog-to-shelf retrieval with no training, against 53.8% for untrained DINOv3. That benchmark used supermarket goods with about nine catalog images per product, so do not present the figure as a forecast for Dyson.

## Rules for the test set

- Each test image's correct answer comes from its source, such as a video title, listing title or caption naming the model. Record `source_url`, `source_title` and `label_basis` for every image. Never label a model by eye: the builder must not write both the answers and the answer key.
- Catalog images and test images come from different sources.
- Include lookalikes, products that are not in the catalog, and multi-product scenes. Wikimedia Commons has labelled Dyson photos (V6, V8, V10, DC models) and two Dyson store photos ("Dyson, Utrecht (2023) 01/02.jpg"). Use the store photos for the multi-product demo even though they have no per-item labels.
- Some frames will show no vacuum. Drop them with an automated "is a vacuum visible" check and record how many were dropped. That check judges presence, never the model.
- Aim for about 30 to 60 labelled test images.

## Report (the Monday deliverable)

Write one HTML page with:

- accuracy before and after: first-choice correct, correct "not in catalog", "not sure" rate and confidently-wrong count;
- the three check models side by side, with cost per photo;
- whether the telling-details variant helped;
- the confusion pairs, such as V12 and V15;
- every test photo with its boxes, answer and the details the model relied on.

Write it in plain, direct English. No marketing tone.

## Step 3 completed on 15 October 2026

Shiva stopped Argon for cost and authorised a cheaper OpenRouter model. The complete baseline is Qwen3.7 Flash: 348 comparisons across all 58 labelled photos, INR 2.91 total (about INR 0.05 per photo), no Qwen request failures. On 57 single-product photos, threshold 80 produced 25 correct decisions and 16 wrong accepted matches; threshold 90 produced 23 correct decisions and 12 wrong accepted matches. Both thresholds had 12 wrong accepted answers with score at least 90. The two-product photo was unresolved. See docs/step-3-baseline.md and out/baseline-qwen/. Argon partial results are separate in out/baseline/; do not present them as full-set accuracy.
